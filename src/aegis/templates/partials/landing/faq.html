Questions people ask

Is this medical advice?

No. AEGIS turns public air-quality data and published guidance into a daily plan. Follow your doctor’s advice and your local health authority’s alerts, and seek care for chest pain or severe breathlessness.

Where do the numbers come from?

Forecasts come from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service through Open-Meteo. Station readings come from OpenAQ, which carries government networks such as India’s CPCB. Google Gemini only writes the briefing text.

What happens to my household details?

They are saved in your browser so you don’t have to retype them. To build a plan they are sent to AEGIS’s server, kept in memory for at most five minutes to write the briefing, and never written to disk or logged. Avoid putting names in the free-text note, because that note is passed to the AI.

Does it work outside India?

Yes. The CAMS forecast covers the whole world. The station card appears wherever OpenAQ has a monitor within {{ config.stationRadiusKm }} km that has reported in the last {{ config.stationRecentHours }} hours.

Why can the forecast and the station disagree?

A station measures one spot, and a busy road or a nearby fire can push it up or down. The forecast estimates an average over a wider area. AEGIS shows both so you can judge, and plans with the forecast because it looks ahead.
